# Bird Map and Saved Locations

BirdCheck's Map is a way to **view and query personal geographic records**. It does not create independent species observations merely by displaying a map marker.

## Using the Map

Open **Map** from Home. BirdCheck can display saved observations, Outing/Sighting locations, and bird-specific saved locations. Tap a marker or geographic grouping to examine its birds and location summary.

A geographic summary may aggregate records within a city or state. A specific named place is narrower than its containing city **when the app has verified that geographic relationship**.

## Locations have a hierarchy

A record may carry several geographic fields:

1. **Country** — e.g., United States
2. **State / administrative area** — e.g., Texas
3. **City / locality** — e.g., a named city
4. **Place** — e.g., Nature Center
5. **Coordinates** — when location has been recorded

If an Outing is verified as being at a place within a city, the observations can contribute to both place and city queries. **A place label alone cannot prove which city it is in.** Two distant places may share the same name.

## Friendly names

BirdCheck can retain recognizable saved-place labels. A friendly name such as “Home” is a label for a saved location, not evidence that a place is in a particular city by itself. When more than one saved location has the same name, a question might require clarification.

## Bird-card known locations

A bird card has **Known Locations**, including a way to **Add Location**. These user-added locations can record where a species was seen independently of a full dated Outing. They can establish species presence at a location, but do not automatically provide a date or individual-bird count.

## Moving a mapped observation

For a recorded bird observation, map editing can retain the original observer coordinates separately when a marker is moved manually. This supports correction without silently rewriting the original GPS evidence. Available map actions depend on the record and screen.

## Why can counts look different?

Compare the **scope** before treating numbers as inconsistent:

- Same geographic place or city?
- Same date range?
- Same sources (dated observations versus undated known locations)?
- Distinct species or known individuals?
- Same local iCloud data on both devices?

Equivalent questions with equivalent filters should use the same underlying evidence and agree. If not, see [Troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md); do not make up a missing location or an extra bird to force the totals to match.
