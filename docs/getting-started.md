# Getting Started with BirdCheck

BirdCheck organizes birding around three ideas: **birds you've seen**, **dated times you observed them**, and **places where you've recorded them**.

## The Home screen

On the iPhone Home screen:

- Tap the **Life List** card to open the personal list of birds recorded as seen.
- Tap **Let's Go Birding** to start a new Outing; the button becomes **Return to Birding** while an Outing is active.
- Tap **New Sighting** to record a bird without starting a full walking Outing.
- Open **Calendar**, **BirdCheck AI**, or **Map** from the Home shortcuts.
- Open the bottom drawer labeled **Outings, Sightings, and Lists** to browse records and lists.
- Open **Settings** for feature preferences, backups, help, and data information.

BirdCheck includes a built-in **Settings → How BirdCheck Works** introduction, which is useful for a quick refresher.

## First-use setup

1. Decide whether you want BirdCheck to use **iPhone Automatic Location**. Location is optional for manual entries, but helpful for routes and maps.
2. Decide whether to enable **iPhone Voice Recording** for spoken input.
3. Consider whether to enable **Automatically Add to Life List** when you record a bird on another list.
4. If you use the Apple Watch, make sure the iPhone and Watch are paired and respond to any requested permissions separately.
5. Start with a Life List entry, a quick Sighting, or your first Outing.

BirdCheck may request permissions when the related feature is first used. Declining a permission can limit that feature without necessarily preventing other recording workflows.

## Three common starting points

**I just saw one bird.** Use **New Sighting**. Record the species, number seen, and whatever location/time information you have.

**I'm going for a birding walk.** Use **Let's Go Birding**, add birds along the way, and finish the Outing when done.

**I saw a bird years ago.** Mark it as seen on your **Life List**, or record a historical outing if you have reliable details. A Life List checkmark should not invent a date or precise individual count.
